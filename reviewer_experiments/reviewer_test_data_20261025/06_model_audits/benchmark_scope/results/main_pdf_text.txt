ABSTRACT
Accurate prediction of delayed nonlinear systems requires a representation of both the current
state and preceding trajectories. This paper proposes a delay-aware latent-memory Koopman-
structured model (DLM-K) for data-driven identification and open-loop prediction. A unidirec-
tional long short-term memory network encodes observed histories and associated inputs when
present. A learned projection of the current state conditions the full recurrent feature sequence
before a Kolmogorov–Arnold lift with Hahn polynomial-based learnable activation functions
constructs nonlinear observables. The current state is also retained explicitly in the lifted repre-
sentationandrecoveredthroughafixedprojection.Afterone-timeinitialization,anattention-free
residualaggregatesrecursivelyavailablelatentstatesbeforepropagationbyasharedlinearcore.
Primary independent simulated tests on a delayed Lorenz–63 system and a continuous stirred-
tank reactor–separator process show that DLM–K has the lowest mean prediction error among
the six compared predictors at the reported prediction steps. Prediction errors still increase at
longer horizons, and separate CSTR diagnostics reveal operating-condition-dependent relative
performance under variations in future inputs and causal histories. The findings support the
proposedlatent-memoryKoopman-structuredpredictoronpreviouslyunusedtrajectoriesunder
the evaluated delayed dynamics.
1. Introduction
Data-driven dynamical models support prediction, monitoring, and model-based decision-making in nonlinear
industrial processes (Korda and Mezić, 2018; Li et al., 2024a). Transport and recycle delays complicate this task
because future evolution depends on preceding trajectories as well as the current state (Han et al., 2024; Qin et al.,
2026).Underthesameprescribedinputs,identicalinstantaneousstatescanthereforeleadtodifferentfutureresponses
when the relevant histories differ. A predictive representation must capture both nonlinear interactions and temporal
dependence. This requirement becomes especially demanding during multi-step open-loop forecasting: once new
observations are unavailable, errors in the initial representation and subsequent updates can accumulate recursively.
Themodelingproblemisthustopreserveusefulhistoricalinformationthroughoutprediction,ratherthanonlyfitlocal,
one-step transitions.
The Koopman framework provides an alternative perspective by representing nonlinear dynamics through linear
evolution in a space of observables (Koopman, 1931). Extended dynamic mode decomposition approximates this
operator using a finite dictionary (Williams et al., 2015), while input-dependent formulations extend Koopman
modeling to externally driven systems (Iacob et al., 2024; Korda and Mezić, 2018; Proctor et al., 2018). Neural
approacheslearntheliftingfunctionsfromdataratherthanprescribethedictionary(Luschetal.,2018;Takeishietal.,
2017); further developments address time-varying dynamics and input-augmented process models (Hao et al., 2024;
Li et al., 2024b). Reduced-order and physics-informed formulations tailor these representations to process prediction
and state estimation (Liu et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023). These advances improve how predictive
coordinates are constructed, but an approximate lifted transition can still accumulate errors when applied recursively.
Fordelayedsystems,therepresentationanditspropagationmustalsoaccountfordependenceonprecedingtrajectories.
Historicalinformationcanbeincorporatedthroughhistory-conditionedinitializationorlatent-memorypropagation
during forecasting. Delay-coordinate models augment the representation with lagged observations, whereas recurrent
encoders summarize temporal dependence from observed histories. LSTM-based Koopman identification with input

initialization to prediction-based memory propagation for delayed dynamics without requiring new state observations
during rollout.
The main contributions are summarized as follows.
1. A unified causal Koopman-structured prediction pathway couples observed-history initialization with predicted-
latent memory propagation. The observed history and current state determine the initial lift, while a rolling buffer
of the initial and recursively predicted latent states conditions subsequent updates without new state observations.
This formulation connects recurrent history representation (Han et al., 2024) with AFT-derived latent-memory
propagation (Nagdi et al., 2026) for delayed-system prediction.
2. A history-conditioned LSTM–HaKAN lift with explicit state retention and a shared linear propagation core
implement this pathway. Relative to HaKAN’s raw-series forecasting formulation (Hasan et al., 2026) and the
transformer–Koopman construction of Zhao et al. (2026), the lift conditions the full recurrent history-feature
sequenceonthecurrentstatebeforeconstructingnonlinearobservables,whileretainingafixedstatereadout.Zero-
gated residual initialization and staged optimization train the coupled lift and latent-memory transition without
re-encoding observations or predicted states during rollout.
3. Independent simulated tests on delayed Lorenz and CSTR systems compare six frozen predictors on previously
unused trajectories. Separate CSTR diagnostics characterize time-averaged accuracy and its dependence on future
inputs and causal histories.

Thus, learned history features can represent delayed dependence without enumerating its terms, but their predictive
sufficiency must be evaluated rather than inferred from network capacity or window coverage.
The Delay–RBF reference in Appendix B instantiates explicit selection: it appends the two delayed Lorenz
coordinatesorthethreedelayedseparatorcoordinatesattheknownlag,thenappliesitsprescribednonlineardictionary.
Thissuppliesaphysicallyinterpretablechoicewhentherelevantvariablesandlagsareknown.Duringrolloutitshifts
a physical-state buffer and re-evaluates the dictionary using predicted states. DLM–K instead encodes the observed
state–input window once; AFT subsequently reads the initial lift and predicted latent states, not newly observed delay
coordinates. Its nonlinear correction is a learned closure, not a proof that𝐸𝜃 spans an invariant subspace. Known
delayed coordinates could also be combined with learned features, but this would require a separately fitted and
validated model; the present comparison establishes no universal preference between explicit and learned history

4.2. Measurement-noise sensitivity and noise-aware identification

added to the CSTR observations. The delayed Lorenz trajectories are deterministic and likewise contain no process
or measurement noise. Thus, the original benchmark does not claim measurement-noise robustness. We therefore

4.4. Discussion and limitations
The independent simulated tests evaluate new trajectories at specified fixed delays. The additional CSTR experi-
mentextendsthisevaluationtothreeseparatelyfitteddelayconditions.Allthreedelaysliewithinthe0.095 hspanofthe
20observedstates.Thismakesthedelayedseparatorstateavailableintherecordedhistory,butcoveringthedelayalone
establishes neither an approximation-error bound for the learned lift nor stability of the nonlinear memory-dependent
recurrence.Predictionaccuracyisthereforesupportedempiricallyforthetestedconditionsandhorizons;aquantitative
guaranteeortransferofafixedpredictortoanunseendelayisnotestablished.Variabilityacrossindependentlytrained
realizationswasnotassessed.Theseparatepost-hocCSTRdiagnosticsdescribecondition-dependentbehaviorandare
not additional independent tests.
The demonstrated generalization is to previously unused trajectories under the stated simulator coefficients,
initialization distributions, sampling rules, and, for CSTR, excitation and process-noise prescriptions. Applying the
samemodelconstructiontoLorenzandCSTRusesseparatelyfittedweights;itdemonstratesreuseoftheidentification
and prediction procedure, without testing transfer of one fitted model between plants. The additional CSTR delay

and history experiments extend the evidence within this process family under the condition-specific fitting protocols
described above. The two examples do not cover multiple independent lags, distributed or time-varying delays,
dependenceondelayedderivatives,actuatororsensordelays,orirregularlysampledandpartiallyobservedprocesses.
Useinthosesettingsrequiresanappropriateinformationrepresentationandindependentidentificationandvalidation.
The reported finite-horizon comparisons consequently support the tested autonomous and input-driven delayed
dynamics, without establishing a uniform accuracy or stability result over general delayed systems.
Stiff dynamics impose separate requirements on simulation and learning. Fast decaying modes can restrict the
stableinternalstepofanexplicitintegratorevenwhentheobservedtrajectoryevolvesslowly.Theobservationinterval
mustalsoresolvethetransientsofinterest:asampledhistorycannotrecoverinformationlostthroughaliasing.DLM–K
learnsasampledtransitionanddoesnotsolvethegoverningdifferentialequationduringprediction,butitstilldepends
onadequatelyresolvedtrainingdata.Iftheobservationintervalisreducedwhilephysicalhistoryandforecastdurations
are retained, more history samples and rollout steps are required. For a strongly stiff target process, data generation
wouldrequireastiffness-appropriatedelaysolverwithtoleranceandstep-refinementchecks,followedbyvalidationat
the intended sampling interval.
TherelevantlocalplantvariationincludesbothcurrentanddelayedJacobians,whereasthefullpredictorvariation
includes the encoder and the nonlinear AFT dependence on its latent context. In particular, for the augmented rollout
state𝝃 𝑘 = vec(𝒛𝑘−𝑀+1∶𝑘 ),errorpropagationdependsontheJacobianofitscompleteupdate𝐺 𝜃 andproductsofthese
Jacobians along a rollout. The spectral radius of𝑨alone therefore establishes neither plant stiffness nor stability of
this update. The numerical and frozen delayed-tangent checks in Appendix B characterize the tested trajectories; the
present benchmarks do not establish accuracy for arbitrary strongly stiff systems.
The comparisons concern complete predictors with different architectures, parameter counts, and optimization
budgets. They do not isolate the marginal effect of current-state conditioning, LSTM encoding, HaKAN lifting, or
AFT correction. The RBF references are frozen closed-form fits and do not receive the neural models’ current-state
projection or staged optimization. No direct comparison with the Koopman+AFT autoencoder of Nagdi et al. (2026)
isreported.Regularizationofthelinearcoredoesnotestablishstabilityofthenonlinearmemory-dependentpredictor.
Thepredictiongainsentailadditionalstorage.Separatinglearnedweights,observed-historybuffers,latentcontext,
and temporary working tensors gives the comparison in Appendix B, Tables B.5 and B.6. At a common float64
precision, the CSTR DLM–K model payload is 1.24MiB and its compact 20-state latent context is 6.41KiB per
prediction origin. The released implementation has a much larger working footprint: the standardized batch-256
probes require 144.97MiB of extra peak allocation for 60-step inference and 2,302.16MiB for a joint 30-step training

Extensiontolargerinteractingsystemsrequiresbalancingrepresentationcapacityagainstdensecomputation.The

5. Conclusions
This study presented DLM–K, a latent-memory Koopman-structured model for multi-step prediction of delayed
nonlinearsystems.Ahistory-conditionednonlinearliftconstructstheinitialpredictiverepresentation,whilerecursively
available latent states inform subsequent transitions without new observations. Recursively available latent states

furthershowthatlowertime-averagederrorcancoexistwithalate-horizondisadvantageunderinput-profilevariation.

meanerroratthesixreportedstepsacrossdelaysof2,5,and10samplingintervals.Furtherworkmayexaminetransfer
to unseen or time-varying delays, wider operating ranges, physical process data, and integration into state estimation

The complementary examples support the reuse of history-conditioned initialization and latent-memory propa-
