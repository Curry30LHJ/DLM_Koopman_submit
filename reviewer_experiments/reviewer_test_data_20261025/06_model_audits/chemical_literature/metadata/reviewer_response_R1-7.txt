Comment:
Finally, there has been a growing body of research on Koopman-based modeling and control in chemical engineering, including applications to nonlinear process systems and MPC. I would encourage the authors to consider citing a few additional studies from the chemical engineering literature to better highlight the relevance of the proposed framework to this community.
https://aiche.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aic.16743
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0098135425003692
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00207179.2021.2013541

Response:
Thank you for these references. We have checked the three suggested studies and added them to the chemical-process modeling and control background in the Introduction. We also connect the noise-identification study to Section 4.2 and the Lyapunov-based control studies to the MPC discussion in Section 4.4. The additions distinguish their identification or control objectives from the delayed-system prediction task evaluated here.

Narasingam and Kwon (2019), Koopman Lyapunov-based model predictive control of nonlinear chemical process systems, combines EDMD-based linear prediction with Lyapunov-based MPC and illustrates chemical reactor stabilization. Narasingam, Son, and Kwon (2023), Data-driven feedback stabilisation of nonlinear systems: Koopman-based model predictive control, uses Koopman eigenfunction coordinates and bilinear controlled dynamics together with a control-Lyapunov-function-based auxiliary controller. These studies provide relevant process-control precedents and show how stability requirements enter controller construction. Their respective control guarantees are not consequences of prediction accuracy alone and are not transferred to the nonlinear history-dependent DLM-K rollout. Our Section 4.4 explains that adapting this design route would require checking its assumptions for the predictor and its feedback reinitialization.

Woelk and Tang (2026), Identification of noisy Koopman models, addresses noisy model identification using an EDMD-like formulation, a likelihood characterization of noise, and regularized block-coordinate estimation of model and noise components; it includes a distillation-column example. We cite this work as an explicit noise-aware identification approach. In Section 4.2, we distinguish fitting such a model from perturbing the observed histories supplied to frozen predictors. Our existing sensitivity audit does not jointly estimate noise components or evaluate their identification procedure.

The Introduction now places these task-specific developments alongside the present focus on observed-history initialization and model-generated latent memory for multi-step delayed-system prediction. All three references are used as methodological and chemical-engineering context. We have not added an unsupported quantitative comparison or claimed to reproduce their noise estimator or controllers.

We use the completed journal issue metadata consistently. The three entries are AIChE Journal 65(11), e16743 (2019); Computers and Chemical Engineering 204, 109366 (2026); and International Journal of Control 96(3), 770-781 (2023). The second article was available online in 2025 and the third in 2022; their DOI year strings do not replace the assigned issue years used in these citations.

The three added paragraphs are blue and appear on PDF viewer pages 2-3, 14, and 23 (printed pages 1-2, 13, and 22). The three new bibliography entries are also blue, on viewer pages 32-33 (printed pages 31-32). The preceding twelve revision rounds and all existing figures, tables, numbered labels, and bibliography entries have been preserved.
