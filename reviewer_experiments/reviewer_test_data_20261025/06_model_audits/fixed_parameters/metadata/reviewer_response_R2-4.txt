Reviewer 2, Comment 4

4.-The parameters involved in eq 1 are said to be fixed. How did you get them and are the valid in general?

Response:

Thank you for requesting clarification of the parameter choices. In the revised manuscript, Eq. (1), x_{k+1} = F(x_k, u_k, H_k), denotes the unknown process map; it does not prescribe a numerical parameter set. The Hahn-basis parameterization is Eq. (6), and the benchmark dynamics are Eq. (19) and Appendix A. We now distinguish the physical coefficients defining the simulated plant, prescribed basis/architecture/optimization settings, quantities estimated from training data, and weights held fixed after identification.

For the Hahn basis, we retain alpha_H = beta_H = 1, N_H = 7 and D_H = 3 from the configuration reported by Hasan et al. (2026, Section 4.1). These values determine the polynomial recurrence; the four expansion coefficients on every connection are learned. N_H is distinct from the degree and from either history or memory length. The same low-degree parameterization is used for both systems and both HaKAN-based predictors. We do not claim that these values are universally optimal; alternative basis settings require target-system validation. We checked the released implementation against the reported recurrence and confirmed the fixed values in both DLM-K models.

The physical coefficients are prescribed for simulation. For Lorenz, the coefficients 10, 28 and 8/3 are the benchmark values, while the delayed terms and tau = 1/6 specify the delayed test system; this delay spans five observations. For CSTR, the balance structure is adapted from Zhang et al. (2023), and Table A.1 reports the retained simulator coefficients and their units. The nominal heat input (2.87, 1.00, 2.87)^T × 10^6 kJ/h matches its first operating input. Our recycle delay, excitation ranges, initialization, perturbation and sampling settings define the present study. The implementation-specific enthalpy conversions remain explicit in Appendix A. These are defined simulation conditions, not estimates obtained by DLM-K or coefficients assumed valid for arbitrary plants. DLM-K receives trajectories rather than the physical coefficients or numerical delay; the Delay-RBF reference separately receives the known lag.

The neural architecture and optimization choices are now distinguished from checkpoint selection. Tables 1 and B.1 specify the adopted configuration: 20 observations, 32 learned observables, a width-16 LSTM, five HaKAN blocks, width-32 patch embeddings, and M = 20. Patch length 5 and stride 2 give eight patches from 19 recurrent outputs. These choices set representation capacity, context and computational cost; they are not presented as an exhaustive-search optimum. Learning rates, loss weights, batch size, budgets and patience are prescribed settings, while validation rollout MSE selects the checkpoint. The unit-weight, unit-threshold Lorenz spectral penalty is a linear-core regularizer, not a stability certificate for the complete recurrence. The separate history experiment examines L_h = 6, 20 and 40 with M fixed, and does not establish universal history or memory lengths.

Several fixed quantities have a different origin. Normalization statistics are estimated using training trajectories and frozen thereafter. C = [I, 0] follows exactly from retaining the state coordinates in the lift, and the causal mask enforces the information constraint. Encoder weights, Hahn coefficients, projections, A, B, AFT weights and the gate are learned; the temporary freezing in staged training is specified in Table B.3. These weights remain fixed during prediction. RBF dictionary size, width, ridge coefficient and delay-coordinate choices are configuration settings, whereas centers and regression matrices are derived from training data, with configuration selection using the validation horizons stated in Appendix B.

Accordingly, the identification framework can be instantiated for another process, but its numerical settings and fitted weights need representative data and validation for that process. The three-delay experiment separately refits the predictors and does not establish transfer of a single frozen model across plant coefficients. The added explanations appear in Section 2.1 (PDF viewer page 4), Section 3.2 (page 6), Section 4.1 (page 10), Appendix A (page 20), and Appendix B (pages 20-21). All additions are blue. The preceding four rounds of revisions and all reported numerical results are preserved.
