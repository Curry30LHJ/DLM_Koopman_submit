Reviewer 1 - Initial submission, 13/10/2026
I appreciate the authors' efforts in developing this interesting framework. The proposed
approach has potential applications in chemical process systems, particularly where time
delays play an important role. I have several questions and suggestions that I hope will
help further strengthen the manuscript.
1. I would be interested to know how the proposed framework could be extended to
larger-scale systems with many interacting states and inputs. It would be helpful if the
authors could discuss potential computational challenges and limitations when applying
the method to more complex systems.
2. In practical chemical processes, it is quite common to encounter multiple time delays
associated with variables measured or sampled at different frequencies. Could the authors
elaborate on how the proposed framework could accommodate such situations? Even if a
numerical demonstration is beyond the scope of the current work, a conceptual discussion
would be valuable for understanding its applicability to realistic chemical processes.
3. This may be a rather fundamental question, but I wonder whether the presence of time
delays introduces additional considerations when selecting observables for the Koopman
framework. Conversely, could an appropriate choice of observables help represent or
handle the delays more effectively? For example, would it be beneficial to treat
individual delayed terms as separate observables and construct the lifted representation
accordingly? I would appreciate the authors' insights into whether such an approach
would be theoretically reasonable or practically useful.
4. I would also be interested in understanding how measurement noise affects the
performance of the proposed method. I fully understand that a comprehensive noise
analysis may be beyond the scope of the present study. However, I suspect that the
performance could depend on the noise level, particularly when delayed measurements
are involved. Could the authors provide some discussion of this issue? Additionally,
might a weak-form formulation or a related noise-robust identification approach be useful
in this context?
5. One potentially interesting application of the proposed framework would be its
integration with MPC. Are there any particular considerations or limitations that should
be taken into account when using the resulting model for prediction and control? For
instance, how might the delays affect prediction accuracy, closed-loop stability, or
computational requirements?
6. Could the authors provide some insight into how the magnitude of the time delay
affects the performance of the proposed method? In particular, are there any conditions
under which reliable prediction or performance can be guaranteed? I understand that
establishing quantitative bounds may be challenging, but even a qualitative discussion of
how performance is expected to change as the delays increase would be very helpful.
7. Finally, there has been a growing body of research on Koopman-based modeling and
control in chemical engineering, including applications to nonlinear process systems and

MPC. I would encourage the authors to consider citing a few additional studies from the
chemical engineering literature to better highlight the relevance of the proposed
framework to this community.

valid in general?
