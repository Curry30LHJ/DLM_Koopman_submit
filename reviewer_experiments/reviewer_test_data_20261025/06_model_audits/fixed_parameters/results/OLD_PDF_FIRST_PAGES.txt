1. Introduction
Transport and recycle delays introduce history dependence into nonlinear process dynamics: systems with the
same instantaneous state can evolve differently because their preceding trajectories differ. Learning a predictive model
therefore requires a representation of both nonlinear interactions and the relevant history. The challenge becomes
more pronounced during open-loop forecasting, when new observations are unavailable and errors in the initial
representation and subsequent updates accumulate recursively. Reliable multi-step prediction is consequently a more
demanding identification objective than a close fit to local, one-step transitions. This issue arises in both autonomous
delayed dynamics and input-driven processes.
The Koopman framework represents nonlinear dynamics through linear evolution in a space of observables [1].
Finite-dimensional approximations include extended dynamic mode decomposition [2] and Koopman formulations
with exogenous inputs [3, 4]. Recent studies have clarified admissible Koopman forms for nonlinear systems with
inputs and have extended neural Koopman learning to time-varying dynamics and input-augmented nonlinear process
models [5–7]. Neural lifting avoids prescribing a fixed dictionary, but recursively applying a finite-dimensional latent
transition can still accumulate approximation error over long horizons [8, 9]. Process-oriented developments include
reduced-order representations and physics-informed models for industrial prediction and state estimation [10–12].
For delayed systems, temporal information can enter the predictor through either its initialization or its recursive
updates. Delay-coordinate models augment the representation with lagged observations, whereas recurrent encoders
summarize temporal dependence from observed histories. LSTM-based Koopman identification with input delay
provides an example of recurrent history encoding [13]. Other approaches combine delay embedding with reduced-
order realization [14] or model lag-aware slow–fast dynamics for industrial anomaly detection [15]. Han et al. [16]
developed a deep recurrent Koopman approach that constructs probabilistic observables from historical state–input
trajectories for uncertain systems with time delays. A complementary approach uses the latent history generated during
prediction. Nagdi et al. [17] introduced attention-free latent-memory corrections before Koopman propagation and also

The initial representation must translate temporal features into observables suitable for recursive prediction.
Kolmogorov–Arnold networks parameterize network connections using learnable univariate functions [18]; temporal
variants combine such representations with sequence processing, frequency decomposition, and patch-based operations
[19–21]. In particular, HaKAN uses Hahn-polynomial mappings and residual intra- and inter-patch operations. Here,
the full LSTM output sequence is retained so that HaKAN can process both local temporal features and interactions
across history segments when constructing the initial observables. Zhao et al. [22] provide a related example of KAN-
based lifting in a transformer–Koopman model for robotic systems. Our design instead uses LSTM-conditioned patches
to initialize a causal latent-memory predictor.
Observed history and predicted latent context serve different roles in delayed-system forecasting. The former
conditions the predictive state at the forecast origin; the latter supplies context for subsequent transitions after new
observations become unavailable. DLM–K combines these roles in one prediction pathway. An LSTM–HaKAN
encoding of the observed state–input history constructs the initial observables, while an AFT-derived residual [17, 23]
operates on the recursively available latent sequence. Unlike the state-encoder and trainable-decoder formulation of
Nagdi et al., the lifted representation retains the current physical state as a prefix, with a fixed projection for state
readout. A shared linear propagation core accommodates either autonomous evolution or prescribed future inputs.
The design objective is thus to connect history-conditioned initialization with subsequent latent-memory updates in a
common formulation for both classes of delayed systems.
The main contributions are summarized as follows.
1. A history-conditioned lifting architecture couples an LSTM encoder with Hahn–KAN patch processing and an
explicit state prefix. It constructs the initial predictive representation from the preceding state–input evolution while
retaining a fixed state readout.
2. A causal rollout formulation couples the history-conditioned lift to an AFT-derived latent-memory residual and an
input-aware linear propagation core. Zero-gated residual initialization and staged optimization train the complete
predictor without re-encoding the observation history during rollout.
3. Independent evaluations on delayed Lorenz and CSTR systems, supplemented by separate CSTR cross-condition
diagnostics, characterize aggregate prediction accuracy and its dependence on forecast horizon, future inputs, and
causal histories.
The comparisons concern complete predictor families. Section 2 formulates the prediction task, Section 3 describes
DLM–K, and Section 4 presents the experiments and their interpretation.
